About Edward D. Clark

Designing systems. Studying collectives. Building what comes next.

I am an enterprise architect, independent researcher, and entrepreneur working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, organizational systems, and collective behavior.

Professional journey

From complex systems to collective intelligence

My career has centered on designing systems that must operate reliably under complexity. Through enterprise architecture, cloud modernization, healthcare interoperability, and artificial intelligence, I have worked to translate difficult ideas into durable technical systems.

That work led to a broader research question: contemporary AI has become increasingly capable of understanding individuals, but can it also infer the latent conditions that emerge within groups?

Collective-State Inference grew from that question. The research examines when computational evidence warrants interpretation as a latent condition of the collective itself.

Research philosophy

Some of the most consequential conditions emerge between people

Many organizational phenomena cannot be explained solely by examining individuals. My work investigates whether artificial intelligence can infer collective conditions that emerge through interaction—and what is required for those inferences to be theoretically valid, empirically supported, and meaningfully attributable to the collective itself.
