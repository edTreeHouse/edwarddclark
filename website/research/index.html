Research agenda

When does an AI inference become a collective-state inference?

My research examines how artificial intelligence can infer latent, emergent conditions of bounded collectives from observable interaction patterns—and what is required for those inferences to be theoretically valid, empirically supported, and appropriately composed across levels.

Research agenda

Collective-State Inference

The program develops the theoretical, operational, and empirical foundations required for defensible collective-state inference.

01

Theoretical foundation

Defines the conditions under which a bounded collective can be treated as the level of inference and establishes how collective-state constructs differ from individual-level and aggregate representations.

Working manuscript · Not peer reviewed. The foundational manuscript was submitted to Computational and Mathematical Organization Theory on August 11, 2026 and declined at editorial screening on August 18, 2026, before external peer review. It remains unpublished and is being prepared for further scholarly evaluation and submission to another venue.

02

Operationalization

Specifies how latent collective conditions can be represented through construct definitions, composition models, evidence windows, indicators, labels, baselines, and candidate inference procedures.

03

Design and validation

Defines the empirical design used to compare aggregate, relational, and candidate collective-state representations and test their construct validity, calibration, and incremental explanatory value.
