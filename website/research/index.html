Research agenda

Understanding collectives as first-class objects of inference

My research examines how artificial intelligence can infer latent, emergent conditions of bounded collectives from observable interaction patterns—without reducing the collective to a simple aggregation of individuals.

Research agenda

Collective-State Inference

The program develops a theoretical foundation, an empirical design and validation strategy, and an operationalization framework for collective-level inference.

01

Theoretical foundation

Defines the collective as a bounded, emergent object of inference and establishes its distinction from individual-level and aggregate constructs.

02

Design and validation

Defines the empirical design used to compare aggregate, relational, and CSI-oriented representations and test their distinct explanatory value.

03

Operationalization

Specifies how latent collective conditions can be represented through evidence windows, relational features, labels, baselines, and inference procedures.
